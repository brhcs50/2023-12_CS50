I purchased a 1975 Corvette in the spring of 2017, and have been working to restore and repair it since.

I prioritized the mechanical and electrical reliability before worrying about the outside appearance. So my first step was the engine. I removed an after market computerized ignition box, which replaced the original distributor. This box fired the cylinders at different intervals than a normal distributor, with the intent to provide more performance. I was concerned that this would damage the engine, especially since I didn't know what condition all of these components were in. Once this box was removed, I installed a new distributor, spark plugs, plug looms, air intake, air filter, water pump, alternator, timing chain, and all new gaskets and seals.

During this process, I noticed there was an exhaust leak from the after market headers, which had not been attached to the engine properly, or had come loose over time. So I completely unbolted the exhaust system from the engine and underside of the car. I then secured the headers to the engine propely with new gaskets, and reattached the exhaust pipes in sections while working twards the rear of the car. This greately improved the sound of the engine and exhaust, and eliminated all leaks and pinging noises.

At this point I shifted my focus to the electrical system. There were no major existing issues in this area, but I had noticed some poorly implemented splices in some areas related to the computerized distributor, which made me concerned for areas I couldn't easily see. So I purchased a new intereior electrical harness, and a new engine harness. I removed the entire interior of the car down to the metal and fiberglass, removed the old harness, and isntalled the new one. I then moved to the engine bay and replaced the engine wiring harness which ended up fixing the oil pressure sending unit.

One headlight would not fully rise up from the hood, which I had assumed would be an electical issue. however, this was actually tied into the vacuum system, which meant there was likely a leak someplace in the vacuum lines. I burned a stick of incense to create smoke in specific areas of the engine bay to determine the source of any vacuum leaks. This resulted in the discovery of several cracked vacuum lines, that when repaired fixed the headlight issue, as well as the air vents in the cabin, and made the engine run more smoothly.

The car was put into storage when I moved to a new city, and unfortunately mice and other animals got into it and chewed up electrical wires and ruined some of the new interior pieces. So, I have lost interest in the project at this point, and will likely sell the car when I get the motivation.
