I started repairing broken video game consoles in my one-bedroom apartment in 2013. I used my dining table as a workbench, beginning by teaching myself soldering with small sections of perfboard and cheap, random components I purchased from Radio Shack.

As I gained confidence, I was able to repair the current gaming consoles at the time, like the PS3 and Wii. I was able to purchase broken consoles from a local thrift store, and then resell the repaired units on eBay. As time went on, I discovered which types of repairs on which consoles would net the most profit, and focused on those. When I moved into my first house, I was able to set up a workshop in the basement, and invested more money into inventory.

I purchased a few Japanese import consoles from when I was a child in the 1980's and 1990's. Through this process I discovered a niche collector group that would pay big money for these types of retro games and consoles. So it was then that I began importing large numbers of broken or dirty gaming consoles from Japanese auction sites. I would then modify them to output in modern video signals like Component or HDMI.

These modifcations required adding additional circuit boards to the consoles, which were normally sold by import stores. But, I had already taught myself how to design simple PCBs with free CAD software when I was repairing PS3 and Wii systems. So, I was able to have my own circuit boards fabricaed by Chinese PCB companies, cutting down on my costs. I was thus able to make a profitable business in this obscure area of retro import gaming consoles.

I still have an active store today, but have cut back significantly since covid.
